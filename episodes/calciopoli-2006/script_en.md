## CHAPTER I — The Most Powerful Man in Italy

S011 · CARD · "The Most Powerful Man in Italy · Chapter I · 1994–2004"

S012 · PAINT · grand office in Turin, heavy curtains, a man in his sixties behind a desk with several phones · OVL name "Luciano Moggi · Juventus General Manager"
> In 1994, Juventus appointed a new general manager. He had never been a star player, or a famous coach.

S013 · PAINT · 1960s small Italian railway station, a young worker in uniform
> He had started out working at a railway station, then became a talent scout, and climbed all the way to the top of Italy's most famous club. His name was Luciano Moggi.

S014 · PAINT · Moggi on the phone, surrounded by notebooks of names
> Moggi had a rare gift. He knew everyone. Agents, coaches, journalists, club presidents… and referees.

S015 · PAINT · three men in suits in a stadium tunnel, long shadows · OVL name "Antonio Giraudo · CEO"
> With chief executive Antonio Giraudo and former club legend Roberto Bettega, Moggi formed what the Italian press called the Triade. Three men running the biggest club in the country.

S016 · PAINT · Juventus trophy cabinet, spotlights on league trophies
> The results were spectacular. League titles, Champions League finals, and transfers that made Juventus the club everyone feared.

S017 · PAINT · packed Serie A stadium, a referee raising a card
> But in Italian football, one question echoed in every café and on every TV talk show. Who chooses the referees?

S018 · PAINT · formal federation meeting room, two older men studying a list · OVL names "Paolo Bergamo · Pierluigi Pairetto · Referee Designators"
> At the time, two men were responsible for assigning referees to Serie A matches: Paolo Bergamo and Pierluigi Pairetto. Every week, they decided who would officiate which game.

S019 · PAINT · close-up of a hand placing a name card on a fixture board
> And in a league where titles could be decided by a single point, the choice of a referee could mean everything.

S020 · MAP · southern Italy, Reggio Calabria marked · OVL place "Reggio Calabria · 6 November 2004"
> On the sixth of November 2004, Juventus lost 2–1 away to Reggina, a small club from the far south of Italy. The referee was Gianluca Paparesta.

S021 · PAINT · Reggina players celebrating in light rain
> It was a famous upset. But the real story happened after the final whistle.

S022 · PAINT · dressing-room corridor, a closed wooden door, a metal key in a suited man's hand, no face

S023 · PAINT · the key on a desk under lamplight
> And the strangest part? Moggi did not say it to a friend. He said it to Maria Grazia Fazi, the secretary of the referees' commission itself.

S024 · PAINT · a secretary's desk with a phone and a referees' fixture list, empty chair

S025 · PAINT · silhouette of a man listening through headphones in a dark room
> What Moggi did not know at the time was that someone was listening.

## CHAPTER II — The Phones Were Listening

S026 · CARD · "The Phones Were Listening · Chapter II · 2004–2006"

S027 · PAINT · prosecutors' listening room, reel recorders turning, a man in headphones
> The story did not begin with referees. It began with a completely different investigation.

S028 · MAP · Italy, Turin then Naples, arrow between them
> In Turin, prosecutors were looking into Juventus, and into a players' agency called GEA World, run by Moggi's son. Between August and September 2004, phones began to be tapped.

S029 · PAINT · Naples prosecutors' office, hundreds of files, two young prosecutors reading transcripts
> The files were later taken up by prosecutors in Naples. And there, the case became something much bigger.

S030 · PAINT · wall of paper transcripts connected with red string
> What investigators found was not one phone call. It was a network. Club executives, referee designators and federation officials, talking to each other, again and again.

S031 · PAINT · a hand taking a small SIM card from a box, faded Swiss flag on the packaging · OVL number "5 · foreign SIM cards"
> When Moggi suspected his phones might be monitored, he turned to a solution: foreign SIM cards, bought in the Swiss border town of Chiasso, and handed to the people he needed to talk to.

S032 · MAP · Italian–Swiss border, Chiasso marked
> Moggi would later say the cards were only for keeping transfers secret, and that they had been the club's idea, not his. Either way, they were not enough.

S033 · MAP · Italy with five red points: Turin, Milan, Florence, Rome, Reggio Calabria · OVL list "Juventus · AC Milan · Fiorentina · Lazio · Reggina"

S035 · PAINT · Rome newsstand at morning, old men reading papers in shock, huge headlines (no legible text) · OVL date "May 2006"

S036 · PAINT · a café, men leaning over a newspaper, espresso cups
> Italians read, in the officials' own words, how referees were discussed… and how things were arranged.

S037 · CARD · OVL number "~100,000 · intercepted conversations"
> This was not one phone call, or ten. The investigations covered tens of thousands of recorded conversations. And what was published was enough to shake an entire country.

S038 · PAINT · Italian TV studio of the 2000s, debate table, a screen showing a replay · OVL name "Il Processo di Biscardi · Italy's most-watched football talk show"

S039 · PAINT · a replay frozen on a studio monitor, an analyst pointing with a pen

The aim, according to investigators: to present the replays in a way that would not expose the referee's mistakes in Juventus's favour.

S040 · PAINT · white-haired host behind a studio desk, the studio lights going dark

S041 · PAINT · Milan office, a man on the phone, window overlooking San Siro · OVL name "Leonardo Meani · AC Milan referee liaison"

S043 · PAINT · executive office, a man in a sharp suit on the phone, Milan pennant behind · OVL name "Adriano Galliani · AC Milan CEO & Lega Calcio President"

S046 · PAINT · Juventus players lifting the league trophy, the crowd's faces uneasy · OVL date "14 May 2006"

S047 · PAINT · Moggi leaving a stadium by a back door in light rain · OVL quote "My soul has been killed." — Luciano Moggi, May 2006

S048 · PAINT · pink sports newspaper front page on a café table, an espresso cup · OVL quote "Juve win their 29th title… or not?" — La Gazzetta dello Sport, May 2006

## CHAPTER III — Berlin

S052 · MAP · Germany, Italy's route: Hanover → Kaiserslautern → Hamburg → Dortmund → Berlin

S058 · PAINT · a bald French player and an Italian defender face to face, referee running toward them, no violence shown

## CHAPTER IV — The Verdict

S065 · CARD · "The Verdict · Chapter IV · July 2006"

S066 · PAINT · sports tribunal in Rome, long table, stern judges, microphones · OVL date "14 July 2006"
> On the fourteenth of July 2006, five days after Berlin, the sports tribunal delivered its verdict.

S067 · CARD · OVL big number "SERIE B · −30 points"
> Juventus: relegated to Serie B for the first time in their history, with a thirty-point penalty, and stripped of the 2005 and 2006 league titles.

S068 · PAINT · club presidents leaving the tribunal under flashbulbs
> Fiorentina and Lazio: relegated too. Milan and Reggina: points deductions.

S069 · PAINT · elegant businessman leaving a prosecutor's office, reporters around him · OVL name "Diego Della Valle · Fiorentina owner"
> Fiorentina's owner, Diego Della Valle, rejected the accusations outright. "The truth is that Fiorentina was the victim of these things… and has absolutely nothing to do with all this."

S070 · PAINT · Juventus fans outside the club offices, lowered flags, angry faces
> The shock was enormous. And the appeals began immediately.

S071 · CARD · list "Final penalties for 2006–07: Juventus — Serie B, −9 · Fiorentina — −15 · Reggina — −11 · AC Milan — −8 · Lazio — −3"
> After two rounds of appeals, the punishments were softened. Fiorentina and Lazio stayed in Serie A with point deductions. In October, Juventus's penalty was cut to nine points… but the relegation stood. And the titles were gone.

S072 · PAINT · an official document stamped with a red seal
> The 2005 title was left without a champion. The 2006 title went to the team that had finished third: Inter Milan. · OVL date "26 July 2006 · 2005–06 title assigned to Inter"

S073 · PAINT · elegant older man with glasses in a grand law office · OVL name "Guido Rossi · FIGC Extraordinary Commissioner, 2006"
> The decision was taken by Guido Rossi, the lawyer appointed to run the federation after the scandal, together with a panel of three experts. The problem? Rossi had once sat on Inter's board of directors.

S074 · PAINT · Juventus fans' banner in an empty stand (no legible text), dusk
> For Juventus fans, that detail alone made the decision suspicious forever. Even the former federation president, Franco Carraro, later said the title should have been taken away… but not given to anyone.

S075 · PAINT · Inter president in a dark suit in a trophy room, blue-and-black flag · OVL quote "The 2006 Scudetto — I feel it is mine." — Massimo Moratti
> Inter's president, Massimo Moratti, put it simply. "The 2006 title… I feel it is mine."

S076 · PAINT · Moggi alone in a dim office, faint window light · OVL name "Luciano Moggi · banned from football"
> Moggi and Giraudo were banned from football for five years. A ban that would later become a lifetime ban.

S077 · PAINT · empty Juventus dressing room, shirts hanging, cold light
> And now, the world champions had to decide. Would they stay with their club in the second division?

S083 · MAP · Italy, Rimini marked · OVL place "Rimini · 9 September 2006"

S085 · PAINT · Del Piero celebrating a goal in the rain at a small ground · OVL number "20 goals · Del Piero, Serie B top scorer 2006–07"

S086 · PAINT · Athens Olympic Stadium, Milan players in red and black lifting the Champions League trophy · OVL date "23 May 2007 · Milan — Champions of Europe"

## CHAPTER VI — Unfinished Business

S087 · CARD · "Unfinished Business · Chapter VI · 2006–today"

S088 · PAINT · criminal courtroom in Naples, colder and more formal, huge files
> The football courts had delivered their verdict. The criminal courts were another story.

S089 · CARD · list "Moggi — the criminal case: 2011 — 5 years 4 months (Naples) · 2013 — reduced to 2 years 4 months (appeal) · 2015 — time-barred (Court of Cassation)"
> In 2011, a court in Naples sentenced Moggi to five years and four months in prison. On appeal in 2013, it was cut to two years and four months. And in March 2015, Italy's highest court ended the case… because the legal time limit had run out.

S090 · PAINT · Moggi, older, walking out of a courthouse, no crowd
> Moggi never went to prison. But his lifetime ban from football remained.

S091 · PAINT · a referee's black kit folded on a courtroom bench · OVL name "Massimo De Santis · the only referee convicted"
> Of all the referees whose names appeared in the case, only one was finally convicted: Massimo De Santis.

S092 · PAINT · Court of Cassation in Rome, marble columns, a thick stamped file · OVL date "9 September 2015 · Court of Cassation — written reasons"
> But even after the case had expired, Italy's highest court published its written reasons in September 2015. And its words were harsh.

S093 · CARD · OVL quote "The creator of an illicit system to condition the matches of the 2004–05 championship." — Court of Cassation, 2015
> It described Moggi as "the creator of an illicit system to condition the matches of the 2004–2005 championship." And it wrote: "More than power, we must speak of an overwhelming power, extending even to journalism and television."

S094 · PAINT · an old tape recorder being played in a courtroom, lawyers listening intently · OVL date "2010"
> Then, in 2010, came a surprise. During the trial, Moggi's defence produced recordings that had not been used in 2006. Calls between executives from other clubs, including Inter, and the referee designators.

S095 · FRAME · portrait of an elderly gentleman in a gold frame on a wall · OVL name "Giacinto Facchetti · Inter President (died September 2006)"

> In one call, in February 2005, Facchetti tells Bergamo the name of the next referee for Inter's match. Bergamo replies: "Tell the coach to stay calm." · OVL quote "Tell the coach to stay calm." — Paolo Bergamo to Giacinto Facchetti, Feb 2005, per published transcripts

S096 · PAINT · warm dinner table in an Italian home, a fish dish, two wine glasses, two empty chairs
> In another, Bergamo invites him to dinner at his home. "We'll eat something together… a little fish… something simple, between friends."

S097 · PAINT · the dinner table now dark and empty, candle burnt out
> Were these innocent calls? Or the same thing Moggi had done? That is exactly the question no court ever answered.

S098 · PAINT · Moggi, older, in a TV interview, studio light · OVL quote "Everyone talked to the designators." — Luciano Moggi's defence
> Moggi summed up his defence in one sentence. "If I call a designator to complain, and the rules allow it… and everyone else who called more than me and asked for favours is left out… then what I did, which is not a crime, becomes a crime."

S099 · PAINT · federation archive, rows of files
> In 2011, federation prosecutor Stefano Palazzi wrote that Inter could have been charged with breaking the rules of sporting fairness. But the facts were already time-barred. And Inter's president at the time, Giacinto Facchetti, had died in September 2006, unable to defend himself.

S100 · PAINT · federation council room, a vote by raised hands · OVL quote "I would have wished for Inter to have renounced the statute of limitations." — Giancarlo Abete, FIGC President, 2011
> On the eighteenth of July 2011, Juventus asked the federation to take the 2006 title away from Inter. The federation's council declared it had no power to do so. Its president, Giancarlo Abete, said he wished Inter had given up its right to the statute of limitations. They did not. The title stayed where it was.

S101 · CARD · OVL number "~€444 million · damages claimed by Juventus — rejected 2016, appeal withdrawn 2023"
> Juventus went to the administrative courts, claiming around four hundred and forty-four million euros in damages. In 2016, the claim was rejected. In 2023, seventeen years after the scandal, Juventus withdrew its last appeal.
