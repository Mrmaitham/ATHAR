## CHAPTER I — The Most Powerful Man in Italy

S011 · CARD · "The Most Powerful Man in Italy · Chapter I · 1994–2004"

S012 · PAINT · grand office in Turin, heavy curtains, a man in his sixties behind a desk with several phones · OVL name "Luciano Moggi · Juventus General Manager"
> In 1994, Juventus appointed a new general manager. He had never been a star player, or a famous coach.

S013 · PAINT · 1960s small Italian railway station, a young worker in uniform
> He had started out working at a railway station, then became a talent scout, and climbed all the way to the top of Italy's most famous club. His name was Luciano Moggi.

S014 · PAINT · Moggi on the phone, surrounded by notebooks of names
> Moggi had a rare gift. He knew everyone. Agents, coaches, journalists, club presidents… and referees.

S015 · PAINT · three men in suits in a stadium tunnel, long shadows · OVL name "Antonio Giraudo · CEO"
> With chief executive Antonio Giraudo and former club legend Roberto Bettega, Moggi formed what the Italian press called the Triade. Three men running the biggest club in the country.

S016 · PAINT · Juventus trophy cabinet, spotlights on league trophies
> The results were spectacular. League titles, Champions League finals, and transfers that made Juventus the club everyone feared.

S017 · PAINT · packed Serie A stadium, a referee raising a card
> But in Italian football, one question echoed in every café and on every TV talk show. Who chooses the referees?

S018 · PAINT · formal federation meeting room, two older men studying a list · OVL names "Paolo Bergamo · Pierluigi Pairetto · Referee Designators"
> At the time, two men were responsible for assigning referees to Serie A matches: Paolo Bergamo and Pierluigi Pairetto. Every week, they decided who would officiate which game.

S019 · PAINT · close-up of a hand placing a name card on a fixture board
> And in a league where titles could be decided by a single point, the choice of a referee could mean everything.

S020 · MAP · southern Italy, Reggio Calabria marked · OVL place "Reggio Calabria · 6 November 2004"
> On the sixth of November 2004, Juventus lost 2–1 away to Reggina, a small club from the far south of Italy. The referee was Gianluca Paparesta.

S021 · PAINT · Reggina players celebrating in light rain
> It was a famous upset. But the real story happened after the final whistle.

S022 · PAINT · dressing-room corridor, a closed wooden door, a metal key in a suited man's hand, no face
> Two years later, Italians would hear a recording of Moggi boasting about what he did after that match. That he had berated the referee… then locked him in his dressing room and walked off with the key. · OVL quote "I locked him in and took the key." — according to an intercepted call

S023 · PAINT · the key on a desk under lamplight
> Moggi later said it had been just a joke, and the related criminal charge was dropped. But the recording survived, and became one of the most infamous moments of the whole affair.

S024 · PAINT · silhouette of a man listening through headphones in a dark room
> What Moggi did not know at the time was that someone was listening.

## CHAPTER II — The Phones Were Listening

S025 · CARD · "The Phones Were Listening · Chapter II · 2004–2006"

S026 · PAINT · prosecutors' listening room, reel recorders turning, a man in headphones
> The story did not begin with referees. It began with a completely different investigation.

S027 · MAP · Italy, Turin then Naples, arrow between them
> In Turin, prosecutors were looking into Juventus, and into a players' agency called GEA World, run by Moggi's son. Between August and September 2004, phones began to be tapped.

S028 · PAINT · Naples prosecutors' office, hundreds of files, two young prosecutors reading transcripts
> The files were later taken up by prosecutors in Naples. And there, the case became something much bigger.

S029 · PAINT · wall of paper transcripts connected with red string
> What investigators found was not one phone call. It was a network. Club executives, referee designators and federation officials, talking to each other, again and again.

S030 · PAINT · a hand taking a small SIM card from a box, faded Swiss flag on the packaging · OVL number "5 · foreign SIM cards"
> When Moggi suspected his phones might be monitored, he turned to a solution: foreign SIM cards, bought in the Swiss border town of Chiasso, and handed to the people he needed to talk to.

S031 · MAP · Italian–Swiss border, Chiasso marked
> Moggi would later say the cards were only for keeping transfers secret. Either way, they were not enough.

S032 · MAP · Italy with five red points: Turin, Milan, Florence, Rome, Reggio Calabria · OVL list "Juventus · AC Milan · Fiorentina · Lazio · Reggina"
> The investigation did not stop at Juventus. Names from Milan, Fiorentina, Lazio and Reggina appeared in the recordings.

S033 · CARD · OVL number "41 · people investigated in Naples"
> In total, magistrates in Naples investigated forty-one people.

S034 · PAINT · Rome newsstand at morning, old men reading papers in shock, huge headlines (no legible text) · OVL date "May 2006"
> Then, in May 2006, everything exploded. Italian newspapers began publishing the transcripts. Page after page. Name after name.

S035 · PAINT · a café, men leaning over a newspaper, espresso cups
> Italians read, in the officials' own words, how referees were discussed… and how things were arranged.

S036 · PAINT · man leaving the federation building through a crowd of photographers
> On the eighth of May, the president of the Italian Football Federation, Franco Carraro, resigned.

S037 · PAINT · empty boardroom, chairs pushed back
> On the eleventh of May, the entire Juventus board resigned.

S038 · PAINT · Juventus players lifting the league trophy, the crowd's faces uneasy · OVL date "14 May 2006"
> And on the fourteenth of May, Juventus won the league title, for the second season in a row.

S039 · PAINT · Moggi leaving a stadium by a back door in light rain · OVL quote "My soul has been killed." — Luciano Moggi, May 2006
> That same day, Luciano Moggi resigned, with a line Italians would never forget. "They have killed my soul."

S040 · PAINT · players celebrating on the pitch, a lone figure walking away in the background
> A title celebrated on the pitch… and a director leaving through the back door.

S042 · PAINT · Italian team bus on a German motorway at dusk
> And through all of this… the players had to travel to Germany. For the World Cup.

## CHAPTER III — Berlin

S043 · CARD · "Berlin · Chapter III · June–July 2006"

S044 · MAP · Germany, Italy's route: Hanover → Kaiserslautern → Hamburg → Dortmund → Berlin
> Italy arrived in Germany in the middle of a storm. At home, news from the courts was taking up more space than news about the team.

S045 · PAINT · Italian team arriving at a hotel, dozens of journalists
> Many expected this team to collapse. How could players focus on a World Cup while their clubs waited for a verdict that could ruin them?

S046 · PAINT · dressing room, white-haired coach in his late fifties speaking to seated players, warm light · OVL name "Marcello Lippi · Italy Coach"
> Coach Marcello Lippi, a man who had spent years in charge of Juventus, decided to turn the pressure into fuel. The message was simple. The whole world is against us. Let's prove them wrong.

S047 · PAINT · blue-shirted defenders blocking a shot, Buffon diving behind them
> And match after match, Italy kept going. A defence that would not break, led by Cannavaro, with Buffon behind him.

S048 · PAINT · Dortmund at night, blue players celebrating · OVL number "2–0 · Italy vs Germany · semi-final"
> In the semi-final, they knocked out the hosts, Germany, in Dortmund, with two goals in the final minutes of extra time.

S049 · PAINT · Berlin Olympiastadion exterior at golden sunset
> And in the final, France. Zidane's France.

S050 · PAINT · a bald French player and an Italian defender face to face, referee running toward them, no violence shown

S051 · PAINT · goalkeepers and players lined up in the centre circle, arms linked

S052 · CLIP · ball striking the crossbar · OVL name "David Trezeguet · France — and Juventus"

S053 · PAINT · Cannavaro lifting the trophy, same moment as S004 from a different angle

S054 · PAINT · Rome at night, streets flooded with flags and fireworks

## CHAPTER IV — The Verdict

S056 · CARD · "The Verdict · Chapter IV · July 2006"

S057 · PAINT · sports tribunal in Rome, long table, stern judges, microphones · OVL date "14 July 2006"
> On the fourteenth of July 2006, five days after Berlin, the sports tribunal delivered its verdict.

S058 · CARD · OVL big number "SERIE B · −30 points"
> Juventus: relegated to Serie B for the first time in their history, with a thirty-point penalty, and stripped of the 2005 and 2006 league titles.

S059 · PAINT · club presidents leaving the tribunal under flashbulbs
> Fiorentina and Lazio: relegated too. Milan and Reggina: points deductions.

S060 · PAINT · Juventus fans outside the club offices, lowered flags, angry faces
> The shock was enormous. And the appeals began immediately.

S061 · CARD · list "Final penalties for 2006–07: Juventus — Serie B, −9 · Fiorentina — −15 · Reggina — −11 · AC Milan — −8 · Lazio — −3"
> After two rounds of appeals, the punishments were softened. Fiorentina and Lazio stayed in Serie A with point deductions. In October, Juventus's penalty was cut to nine points… but the relegation stood. And the titles were gone.

S062 · PAINT · an official document stamped with a red seal
> The 2005 title was left without a champion. The 2006 title went to the team that had finished third: Inter Milan. · OVL date "26 July 2006 · 2005–06 title assigned to Inter"

S063 · PAINT · Moggi alone in a dim office, faint window light · OVL name "Luciano Moggi · banned from football"
> Moggi and Giraudo were banned from football for five years. A ban that would later become a lifetime ban.

S064 · PAINT · empty Juventus dressing room, shirts hanging, cold light
> And now, the world champions had to decide. Would they stay with their club in the second division?

## CHAPTER V — Serie B

S065 · CARD · "Serie B · Chapter V · 2006–2007"

S066 · PAINT · press conference, a tall dark-haired defender announcing a move, many cameras · OVL number "Ballon d'Or 2006 · Fabio Cannavaro"
> Some left. Cannavaro, captain of the world champions, joined Real Madrid, and at the end of the year he won the Ballon d'Or. Zambrotta left for Barcelona. Ibrahimović and Vieira went to Inter.

S067 · PAINT · Buffon, Del Piero, Nedvěd and Trezeguet standing together on the pitch, heroic group portrait
> But others stayed. Buffon, the best goalkeeper in the world. Del Piero, the club captain. Nedvěd, the 2003 Ballon d'Or winner. And Trezeguet, the man who had missed in Berlin.

S068 · PAINT · Del Piero alone in an empty stadium at dusk, looking at the stands · OVL quote "A true gentleman never leaves his lady." — attributed to Alessandro Del Piero
> Asked why he stayed, Del Piero is credited with a line that became legend. "A true gentleman never leaves his lady." And the play on words was intentional. In Italy, Juventus are known as the Old Lady.

S069 · MAP · Italy, Rimini marked · OVL place "Rimini · 9 September 2006"
> On the ninth of September 2006, Juventus played their first ever match in Serie B. Away, in Rimini. It finished 1–1.

S070 · PAINT · small stadium, half-full stands, Juventus players on a modest pitch
> World champions… in stadiums that held fewer than ten thousand people.

S071 · PAINT · Del Piero celebrating a goal in the rain at a small ground · OVL number "20 goals · Del Piero, Serie B top scorer 2006–07"
> But the team did not break. They started the season nine points behind, and finished it in first place. Del Piero was the league's top scorer with twenty goals. Juventus went straight back up.

S072 · PAINT · Athens Olympic Stadium, Milan players in red and black lifting the Champions League trophy · OVL date "23 May 2007 · Milan — Champions of Europe"
> And the biggest twist of all: Milan, one of the punished clubs, who started their season eight points down and had to play a qualifying round at the end of August… won the Champions League in Athens in May 2007, with two goals from Filippo Inzaghi against Liverpool.

## CHAPTER VI — Unfinished Business

S073 · CARD · "Unfinished Business · Chapter VI · 2006–today"

S074 · PAINT · criminal courtroom in Naples, colder and more formal, huge files
> The football courts had delivered their verdict. The criminal courts were another story.

S075 · CARD · list "Moggi — the criminal case: 2011 — 5 years 4 months (Naples) · 2013 — reduced to 2 years 4 months (appeal) · 2015 — time-barred (Court of Cassation)"
> In 2011, a court in Naples sentenced Moggi to five years and four months in prison. On appeal in 2013, it was cut to two years and four months. And in March 2015, Italy's highest court ended the case… because the legal time limit had run out.

S076 · PAINT · Moggi, older, walking out of a courthouse, no crowd
> Moggi never went to prison. But his lifetime ban from football remained.

S077 · PAINT · an old tape recorder being played in a courtroom, lawyers listening intently · OVL date "2010"
> Then, in 2010, came a surprise. During the trial, Moggi's defence produced recordings that had not been used in 2006. Calls between executives from other clubs, including Inter, and the referee designators.

S078 · FRAME · portrait of an elderly gentleman in a gold frame on a wall · OVL name "Giacinto Facchetti · Inter President (died September 2006)"
> In 2011, federation prosecutor Stefano Palazzi wrote that Inter could have been charged with breaking the rules of sporting fairness. But the facts were already time-barred. And Inter's president at the time, Giacinto Facchetti, had died in September 2006, unable to defend himself.

S079 · PAINT · federation council room, a vote by raised hands · OVL quote "I would have wished for Inter to have renounced the statute of limitations." — Giancarlo Abete, FIGC President, 2011
> On the eighteenth of July 2011, Juventus asked the federation to take the 2006 title away from Inter. The federation's council declared it had no power to do so. Its president, Giancarlo Abete, said he wished Inter had given up its right to the statute of limitations. They did not. The title stayed where it was.

S080 · CARD · OVL number "~€444 million · damages claimed by Juventus — rejected 2016, appeal withdrawn 2023"
> Juventus went to the administrative courts, claiming around four hundred and forty-four million euros in damages. In 2016, the claim was rejected. In 2023, seventeen years after the scandal, Juventus withdrew its last appeal.

## THE PRICE — Ending

S082 · PAINT · Turin today, modern stadium, fans in black and white, calm wide shot
> Twenty years have passed since the summer of 2006. And in Italian cafés, the same old question is still being asked. Who chooses the referees?

S083 · PAINT · split composition: Cannavaro lifting the World Cup / a court file
> For Juventus fans, Calciopoli was an injustice. For their rivals, it was justice that came too late. And for the courts, most of the cases ended not with acquittal, and not with final conviction… but simply with time running out.

S084 · PAINT · back in Berlin: the golden trophy alone on a podium, stadium lights slowly fading
> But one thing is beyond dispute. In a single summer, Italy lifted the World Cup… and brought down its greatest club.

> And in Italian football, nothing was ever quite the same after that summer.
